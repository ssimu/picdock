Google Photos on an
Android tablet frame in 2026

Why frame apps stopped showing your Google Photos albums, what still works, and when to choose something else.

September 2026 · 7 min read

Disclosure up front. We make PicDock, the Android app used in the setup below, so we are not neutral. Every PicDock detail here was checked against the source code of the released version (1.8.8) in September 2026. Every statement about Google's APIs links to Google's own pages. We also say plainly when another product fits better.

If you set up a tablet frame a few years ago, you may have picked a Google Photos album once and watched new photos arrive by themselves. Then, sometime in 2025, many of those apps stopped showing albums, lost the album list, or asked you to "select photos" instead. That was not the apps breaking one by one. Google changed the rules for every third-party app at the same time.

Short answer: since March 31, 2025, ordinary apps cannot browse your Google Photos library or albums. They can only show photos you hand-pick in Google's picker. Auto-updating albums are now reserved for Google's own devices and for frame makers Google accepts as partners. If you want a tablet that picks up new photos on its own, a synced folder (OneDrive, Dropbox or a NAS) is the reliable route.

What Google changed, and when

On September 18, 2024 Google announced "Updates to the Google Photos APIs: Picker API launch and Library API changes". The key points, from Google's API updates page:

  • Effective March 31, 2025.
  • The scopes photoslibrary.readonly, photoslibrary.sharing and photoslibrary are removed. API calls that rely only on them return 403 PERMISSION_DENIED.
  • Listing, searching and reading are limited to albums and media items created by the app itself. Shared-album features were removed from the API.
  • For choosing photos from the whole library, Google points developers to the new Google Photos Picker API.

So a frame app can no longer say "show album Grandkids" and re-read it every hour. The album belongs to you, not to the app, and the app is no longer allowed to look inside.

The two ways apps can still show Google Photos

1. The Picker API: you choose, the app receives copies

The app opens Google's own picker (in the Google Photos app or the browser). You browse or search your library, including your albums, and select items. The app then receives those items, not a link to an album. Two limits from Google's documentation shape every Picker-based frame:

  • At most 2,000 items per picking session (sessions reference, maxItemCount).
  • Download links last 60 minutes (media items guide), so the app has to save the photos right away to show them later.

The consequence: when someone adds a new photo to that album next week, the app has no way to know. You pick again.

2. The Ambient API: approved partners only

Google also has an Ambient API for "ambient devices like smart TVs and photo frames", where you choose albums in the Google Photos app and the device keeps showing them. It is not open to everyone: Google's partner program page says "To get access to the Ambient API, you must first be accepted into the partner program." Frames that advertise live Google Photos albums in 2026 are either Google's own devices or accepted partners.

Which option fits you

  • You want a Google Photos album that updates itself, with no extra steps. Use Google's own screens (Google TV and Nest Hub photo modes show Google Photos) or a partner frame. Aura, for example, brought back Google Photos support in June 2026 (9to5Google), and its help page describes auto-adding albums. That means buying their hardware; we have not tested it.
  • You want a spare Android tablet to show a hand-picked set of Google Photos, even offline. A Picker-based app works well. Setup below.
  • You want a spare Android tablet that picks up new family photos on its own. Put the frame photos in a synced folder instead of a Google Photos album. See the synced-folder route.

Setup: Google Photos on an Android tablet with PicDock

You need an Android tablet or phone (Android 7.0 or later) and PicDock from Google Play (store name: "Photo Slideshow, Digital Frame"). PicDock is not the macOS app picdock.app, which is an unrelated product. The Google Photos source is in the Google Play version only.

  1. Open PicDock → Photo sources → Google Photos → Add photos.
  2. Choose your Google account and allow access. PicDock asks only for the Picker permission (photospicker.mediaitems.readonly): it can read the items you select, nothing else in your library.
  3. Google's picker opens. Select photos (you can open an album and select from it), tap Done and come back to PicDock. PicDock waits up to 10 minutes for you to finish.
  4. PicDock downloads the selected photos to the tablet, sized to at most 2560 pixels on the long side, and shows "N photos added".
  5. Tap Play all saved photos. If another source is already showing, choose Replace or Add to current to mix them with photos on the tablet, OneDrive, Dropbox, an iCloud shared album or a NAS.

Then keep the tablet on while charging. Our always-on tablet guide covers screen-on, night hours and starting when the charger is connected.

How PicDock handles picked photos

  • Saved on the tablet, no expiry. Picked photos stay in the app's own storage until you remove them (long-press in the Google Photos manage screen) or uninstall the app. They play without Wi-Fi. When the saved set passes 500 MB, the manage screen suggests removing photos you no longer need.
  • Removing is local. Removing a photo from the frame does not touch Google Photos.
  • Duplicates skipped. Picking the same album again adds only photos that are not saved yet, so "re-pick the whole album" is a safe way to top up.
  • Photos only. If you select only videos, PicDock says videos cannot be added. Videos stored on the tablet itself do play.

What PicDock cannot do with Google Photos

Plainly, as of the released version 1.8.8 (September 2026):

  • No automatic updates from Google Photos. Photos added to an album later do not appear until you pick them. PicDock refreshes other sources on a schedule, but a picked set has nothing to refresh from.
  • No album or full-library browsing inside the app. Google's policy does not allow it; you browse inside Google's picker.
  • No shared-album following. Shared albums can be browsed in the picker like any album, but the frame gets copies, not the album.
  • No videos from Google Photos.
  • Not a Google Photos partner frame. PicDock uses the public Picker API, not the Ambient API.

The synced-folder route: new photos appear by themselves

If "grandma's tablet shows new photos without anyone touching it" is the goal, the honest answer in 2026 is to stop depending on a Google Photos album. Keep a frame folder somewhere a frame app is still allowed to re-read:

  • OneDrive folder: PicDock re-reads a chosen folder every 30 minutes. OneDrive folder guide.
  • Dropbox folder: also every 30 minutes. Dropbox folder guide.
  • NAS share (Synology and other SMB shares): every 15 minutes. NAS photo frame guide.
  • iCloud shared album for iPhone families: every 15 minutes. iCloud shared album guide.

In each case PicDock also checks when you come back to the app (at most once every 5 minutes), and a failed or suspicious refresh keeps the current photo list instead of emptying the frame. Family members add photos to the folder from their own phones, and the tablet follows.

FAQ

Why can't photo frame apps show my Google Photos albums anymore?

Google removed the Library API read scopes on March 31, 2025 (announced September 18, 2024). Apps can only read what they uploaded themselves. They now use the Picker API, where you select items by hand, or join Google's partner program for the Ambient API.

Can an Android tablet frame show a Google Photos album that updates automatically?

Only via Google's own devices or an approved partner using the Ambient API. Picker-based apps, including PicDock, get the items you pick, so new album photos must be picked again.

How many Google Photos can I pick at once?

Up to 2,000 per picking session (the Picker API maximum). In PicDock you can pick again as often as you like; already-saved photos are skipped.

Are picked Google Photos kept on the tablet?

Yes. Google's links expire after 60 minutes, so PicDock saves the photos immediately and keeps them until you remove them or uninstall. They play offline.

Does PicDock show videos from Google Photos?

No. Its Google Photos source saves photos only. Videos stored on the tablet do play.

What is the best way to get a self-updating frame on Android?

A Google Photos partner product, or a synced folder: OneDrive or Dropbox (every 30 minutes in PicDock), or a NAS share (every 15 minutes).

In short

Since March 31, 2025, apps cannot follow your Google Photos albums. On a spare Android tablet you can still show hand-picked Google Photos (up to 2,000 per pick, saved on the tablet, photos only), topping up by picking again. For albums that update themselves, use Google's own devices or a partner frame. For a tablet that follows new family photos on its own, use a OneDrive, Dropbox or NAS folder.
