A Dropbox folder on an
Android tablet frame

How to turn an Android tablet into a photo frame that shows a Dropbox folder and picks up new photos by itself — and what does not work.

September 2026 · 6 min read

Disclosure up front. We make PicDock, the Android app used in the steps below, so we are not neutral. Every PicDock detail here was checked against the source code of the released version (1.8.8) in September 2026, we list what it cannot do with Dropbox, and we name a Dropbox-only alternative.

Plenty of families already keep their photos in Dropbox: camera uploads from a phone, a shared folder after a trip, scans of old prints. And there is often a spare Android tablet in a drawer. The Dropbox app on that tablet is a file browser, not a frame — it will not sit on a shelf and cycle through a folder all day, and it will not show new photos unless someone taps.

Short answer: use a frame app that signs in to Dropbox. Put the frame photos in one Dropbox folder, sign in once on the tablet, pick that folder, and leave it running. New photos added to the folder show up on their own.

What you need

  • A Dropbox account with the photos in it.
  • An Android tablet or phone running Android 7.0 or later.
  • PicDock (Google Play name: "Photo Slideshow, Digital Frame"). PicDock is not the macOS app picdock.app — that is an unrelated product.

Step 1 — In Dropbox: make a frame folder

  1. On your computer, on dropbox.com or in the Dropbox phone app, create a folder at the top level of your Dropbox, for example Frame.
  2. Copy or move the photos you want on the frame into it. Subfolders are fine — when you choose a folder, PicDock also reads every folder inside it.

Why the top level? PicDock's folder list shows the folders directly in your Dropbox root. Deeper folders are not listed on their own; they are included through their parent. (Picking Camera Uploads works too, if you want every phone photo on the frame.)

Step 2 — On the tablet: sign in and pick the folder

  1. Open PicDock → Photo sources → Dropbox.
  2. Dropbox's own sign-in page opens. Sign in and allow access. PicDock asks only for permission to read file contents; it does not ask to change or delete anything in your Dropbox. You stay connected, so this is a one-time step. If the sign-in does not complete, try again.
  3. Tick the folder (you can tick several). "All Photos" uses the whole account instead — possible, but slower to scan and refreshed less often, so a folder is the better choice for a frame.
  4. If something is already showing, choose Replace (only Dropbox) or Add to current (mix Dropbox with photos on the tablet, Google Photos, OneDrive, an iCloud shared album or a NAS).

Then set the tablet to stay on while charging and put it on the shelf. Our always-on tablet guide covers keeping the screen on, night hours and starting when the charger is connected.

How new photos reach the frame

A frame on a shelf is never touched, so the refresh interval decides when new photos appear. In the released version, PicDock re-reads Dropbox while the slideshow runs:

  • A chosen folder: every 30 minutes. (Several folders: also 30 minutes.)
  • Whole account ("All Photos"): every 2 hours, because it has to walk the entire Dropbox.
  • When you come back to the app, it checks at the next photo change, at most once every 5 minutes. So the usual "I just added photos, let me look at the frame" moment works without waiting.

Deleted photos are removed from the frame on the same schedule. If Dropbox does not answer, or a scan suddenly looks like more than half the photos vanished with nothing new, the frame keeps its current list instead of emptying itself — a hiccup is not treated as "everything was deleted". The checks run while the slideshow is running; a paused or closed app does not refresh.

Sharing one folder with the family

The frame shows folders in the signed-in account's Dropbox. The simplest family setup: the frame's account owns the Frame folder and shares it from Dropbox with edit access, so relatives can drop photos into it from their own devices. Their uploads land in the owner's folder, and the frame picks them up at the next refresh. We have not separately tested folders that someone else owns and shared with you, so keep the frame folder in the frame account.

Offline and caching

Photos are downloaded as they come up and kept in an on-device cache — 500 MB by default, adjustable in Photo sources (100 MB, 250 MB, 500 MB, 1 GB, 2 GB or unlimited), where you can also clear it. When the cache is full, the least recently used photos are removed first. Photos already in the cache can be shown again without the network; photos that were never downloaded cannot. There is no "download the whole folder for offline" switch, so a frame that is regularly offline is better served by copying the photos to the tablet itself.

What PicDock cannot do with Dropbox

Plainly, as of the released version 1.8.8 (September 2026):

  • No videos from Dropbox. The Dropbox source shows photos only (JPG, PNG, GIF, BMP, WebP, and HEIC/HEIF on Android 9 or later); videos and other files in the folder are skipped.
  • Only top-level folders in the picker. Put the frame folder at the top level, or pick its parent.
  • Not instant. New photos appear within about 30 minutes for a folder, not the moment you upload them — unless you open the app, which checks right away.
  • No full offline copy (see above).

Another honest option: dfolio

dfolio ("dfolio - Dropbox™ Photos") by Snapwood Apps is a Dropbox-only gallery and frame app. Its Google Play listing (checked September 24, 2026) describes it as a "fast gallery and beautiful digital photo frame", says "auto-refresh keeps the slideshow up to date when you add new photos", lists "Play Dropbox movies and videos", and mentions an Android TV / Google TV app and Chromecast. The listing shows in-app purchases, 50K+ downloads and a 4.3-star rating, and says it is not affiliated with Dropbox. We have not tested it, so read the listing yourself — but if Dropbox videos or a TV app matter more to you than mixing sources, it is worth a look.

PicDock's case is the other way round: Dropbox is one of several sources you can mix on the same frame, plus the clock, weather, night sleep and charging auto-start that a tablet on a shelf needs. FAQ

Can an Android tablet show a Dropbox folder as a photo frame?

Yes. The Dropbox app is a file browser, but frame apps that sign in to Dropbox can play a folder full screen. In PicDock: Photo sources → Dropbox, sign in, tick the folder.

Do new photos added to Dropbox appear automatically?

Yes. While the slideshow runs, PicDock re-reads a chosen folder every 30 minutes (the whole account every 2 hours) and also checks when you return to the app, at most once every 5 minutes. Deleted photos are removed too.

Are photos in subfolders shown?

Yes. Choosing a folder includes every folder inside it. The picker lists only top-level folders.

Are videos in Dropbox shown?

Not in PicDock. The Dropbox source shows photos only and skips videos. dfolio's listing says it plays Dropbox videos, if that matters to you.

Does the frame keep working if the Wi-Fi drops?

Partly. Photos already shown stay in the cache (500 MB by default) and the photo list is kept through a failed refresh, but photos that were never downloaded wait for the connection to return.

In short

Dropbox family, spare Android tablet: make a top-level Dropbox folder for the frame, sign in once, pick the folder, and leave the slideshow running. New photos appear within about 30 minutes, or right away when you open the app. Photos only, not videos.
