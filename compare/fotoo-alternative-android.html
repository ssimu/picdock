Best Fotoo alternatives for
Android photo frames (2026)

Seven options compared from their own listings — PicDock, PhotoFrame 2, Fravora, Pixette, Snapwood apps, Frameo and FrameOS — and when Fotoo is still the better choice.

September 2026 · Updated October 25, 2026 · 9 min read

Disclosure up front. We (DD_studio) make PicDock, one of the apps on this list, so we are not neutral. To keep this fair, every detail about Fotoo and the other apps comes from that app's own Google Play listing (US English) or official website, checked October 25, 2026, and every PicDock detail from our public fact sheet. We have not lab-tested the other apps, and we say plainly where another app is the better pick.

Fotoo - Photo Frame Slideshow by Bopp Studio has been on Google Play since May 12, 2014, with 1M+ downloads and a 3.83-star rating from 6,758 ratings; its last update was September 21, 2026. It reads device photos, Google Photos, Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive and Samba/SMB. If you are looking for something else — a different price model, a source Fotoo does not list, or a self-hosted setup — here are the alternatives people most often compare it with.

Short answer

  • Photos in Google Drive: stay with Fotoo, or look at PhotoFrame 2, which also lists Google Drive.
  • Mixing cloud, NAS and iCloud shared albums, with a clock, weather, alarm and sleep schedule: PicDock (ours).
  • Self-hosted servers (Immich, WebDAV, Synology Photos): Pixette or Fravora; PhotoFrame 2 also lists Immich and WebDAV.
  • All photos in one cloud service and you want an Android TV app: Snapwood's PixFolio (Google Photos), SkyFolio (OneDrive) or dfolio (Dropbox).
  • Buying a frame for a relative instead of reusing a tablet: Frameo.
  • Building a DIY e-ink or Raspberry Pi frame: FrameOS (not an Android app).

The alternatives, one by one

1. PicDock — mixed sources plus a clock, weather and alarm (ours)

PicDock is listed on Google Play as "Photo Slideshow, Digital Frame" by DD_studio (package kr.devdad.picdock); it is not the macOS app picdock.app, which is an unrelated product. It plays device photos and videos, Google Photos (via the official picker), Dropbox, OneDrive, iCloud shared albums and NAS/SMB shares, and can mix several of them in one slideshow. Cloud and NAS folders are re-read at regular intervals, so new photos appear without touching the tablet. On top of the photos it shows a clock and live weather, and it has an alarm with snooze, night dimming, a sleep schedule that can turn the screen off, auto-start when the charger is connected, and a dual mode that shows two portrait photos side by side. An on-device "only photos with people" filter is free; a pet filter and automatic person grouping are Premium.

Where Fotoo is better: Google Drive, a much longer track record (since 2014) and a far larger user base (1M+ vs 10K+). Fotoo's latest update also announces Google Photos Ambient support, while PicDock's Google Photos source is a picker: picked photos are saved on the device and new ones have to be picked again.

Cloud and NAS sources are in the Android app only. The iPhone/iPad app ("PicDock: Digital Photo Frame") is a separate app that uses the device's Photos library.

2. PhotoFrame 2 — the longest source list, including Google Drive

PhotoFrame 2 – Photo Slideshow by Jeroen Wyseur (be.wyseur.photo2) lists more than 20 photo sources: local folders, USB/OTG drives, Google Photos including shared albums, Dropbox, OneDrive, Google Drive, WebDAV (Nextcloud, ownCloud, Synology), Immich, SFTP, pCloud, Flickr and NAS/SMB, plus account-free "Discover" sources such as Unsplash, Pexels, NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day and museum collections. Two sources can be combined for free; Premium unlocks unlimited multi-source slideshows. Videos play inline with photos. Casting to a Chromecast TV, scheduling, the Ken Burns transition and backup/restore are Premium, which the listing sells as a single unlock (not yearly or monthly), with ad-free and bundle options. The listing shows "Contains ads" and in-app purchases of $0.49–$5.99 per item.

Where it beats PicDock: Google Drive, WebDAV, Immich, SFTP, pCloud and Flickr, plus Chromecast casting. Things to weigh: 1K+ downloads and no rating shown yet; the website linked from the listing (phealit.be/photoframe2) returned a 404 when we checked.

3. Fravora — NAS, cloud, radio and voice control

Fravora: Digital Photo Frame by aniujual software (com.aniujual.fravora) plays local folders, Synology and QNAP NAS over SMB, Google Photos, Microsoft OneDrive, Dropbox and Immich in one slideshow, and works fully offline with local or NAS sources. It adds built-in internet radio, live weather, a clock and photo info overlays, running-hours scheduling with a standby clock, on-device voice control, hidden photos and anti-burn-in shifts. The latest update adds photo+video albums and music from local folders and SMB. The free version includes local, SMB and cloud sources, the clock, radio and scheduling; Premium adds weather, more transitions, voice control and custom schedules. The listing shows "Contains ads" (it says the only ad is an optional rewarded ad) and in-app purchases of $5.99 per item. Fravora's website also links an Amazon Appstore version.

Where it beats PicDock: Immich, internet radio and voice control. Things to weigh: 100+ downloads, no rating shown, last updated August 21, 2026.

4. Pixette — for self-hosted photo libraries

Pixette – Slideshow for NAS by Mischa (app.moleson.pixette) streams straight from your own storage: WebDAV (Nextcloud, ownCloud, Synology, TrueNAS), Synology Photos albums, Immich, SMB/CIFS and on-device photos, with OneDrive added as a beta in the latest update. It indexes very large libraries (the listing says 2 million+ files), centers photos on faces, filters by date range, orientation or a location you pick on a map, and shows a clock, weather with a 3-day forecast and a sleep/wake schedule. It supports Android 7.0+ and Android TV/Google TV. It is free for up to 500 files; an annual subscription unlocks unlimited files and premium features (the listing shows a $7.99 in-app item). Pixette's website says it is also available for iPhone, iPad and Apple TV, and adds Jellyfin and Plex.

Where it beats PicDock: WebDAV, Immich, Synology Photos albums, location filters and Apple devices. Things to weigh: 500+ downloads on Google Play; the free tier stops at 500 files.

5. PixFolio, SkyFolio and dfolio (Snapwood Apps) — one app per cloud

Snapwood makes a separate frame app for each service: PixFolio for Google Photos (1M+ downloads, 3.78 from 5,327 ratings), SkyFolio for OneDrive (100K+, 4.30 from 1,199) and dfolio for Dropbox (50K+, 4.49 from 741). Their listings mention clock, weather, photo and video slideshows, Android TV and Chromecast, with in-app purchases of $3.99–$34.99 per item; all three were updated in October 2026. A good fit if all your photos are in one of those services and you want a TV app.

6. Frameo — a different kind of product

"Frameo: Share to photo frames" is not a tablet app: its listing says it "only works with official Frameo WiFi photo frames" — you send photos and 15-second videos from your phone to a hardware frame. 5M+ downloads and 4.77 stars from 148,725 ratings, the most of anything here. Frameo+ is $1.99/month or $16.99/year and Premium $7.99/month or $69.99/year (frameo.com/subscriptions). Good if you want to buy a frame for a relative rather than reuse a tablet; see our Frameo alternatives page.

7. FrameOS — open source, for DIY frames

FrameOS is AlternativeTo's top-ranked Fotoo alternative, but it is a different category: free, open-source software (AGPL-3.0, since 2023) that turns a Raspberry Pi or ESP32 plus an e-ink, HDMI or LCD display into a calendar, dashboard or art frame. Everything renders on the device; you can run it standalone, from a self-hosted backend, or via FrameOS Cloud (free for personal use). It is not an Android app, so it will not run on a spare tablet — but if you want to build a frame from scratch, nothing else here comes close.

When to stay with Fotoo

  • Your photos are in Google Drive and you do not want to change apps (PhotoFrame 2 is the only alternative here that also lists it).
  • You value a long track record: on Google Play since 2014, updated September 21, 2026.
  • You want an app many people already use: 1M+ downloads and 6,758 ratings mean more answers online. Every Android alternative here has far fewer installs.
  • It already works for you. A frame that runs fine is worth keeping; switching costs an evening of setup.

What PicDock cannot do

  • No Google Drive. Use Fotoo or PhotoFrame 2 if your photos are there.
  • No video playback from cloud sources. Videos play from device storage (and, as an opt-in beta, from NAS/SMB shares). Videos in Google Photos, Dropbox, OneDrive or iCloud do not play.
  • Google Photos does not update itself. The picker saves the photos you choose on the device; new photos have to be picked again.
  • No WebDAV, Immich or Chromecast casting. See Pixette, Fravora or PhotoFrame 2.
  • The screensaver (Daydream) shows device photos only. Cloud and NAS sources play in the full-screen slideshow, which can start when the charger is connected.
  • Cloud and NAS sources are Android-only. The iOS app uses the device's Photos library.

Switching from Fotoo: what to expect

None of these apps import Fotoo settings. Your photos do not move, though: they stay in the same cloud account, NAS or device folder, so you only reconnect.

  1. Note what Fotoo shows today: accounts, albums and folders, the interval, and whether it starts as a screensaver or at a scheduled time.
  2. Install the new app on the same tablet and keep Fotoo installed while you compare; just do not set both to auto-start.
  3. Reconnect each source and re-create the interval, transition, clock and weather. In PicDock this is Photo sources → sign in to Dropbox or OneDrive, enter the NAS host and share for SMB (NAS guide), or use the Google Photos picker; Add to current mixes several sources.
  4. Run both for a few days before uninstalling Fotoo, and check whatever mattered most (for example, whether new photos in a shared folder appear on their own).

Fotoo purchases do not carry over; refunds are between you, Bopp Studio and Google Play.

What PicDock costs

The Google Play version is free to download and usable without paying: all photo sources except web upload are free, along with video playback, the face filter, dual mode, night mode, alarm, clock and current weather. New installs get a 7-day trial of every feature. Optional Premium adds all 30+ transitions, forecasts, clock designs, the pet filter and person grouping, and removes ads; it is sold as lifetime (US$19.99), yearly or monthly, with prices shown in the app and varying by country. Ads never appear on the slideshow itself; in the free version a banner can appear in settings screens. The Amazon Appstore edition for Fire tablets is a US$4.99 paid app. Full details: PicDock facts page.

FAQ

What is the best alternative to Fotoo on Android?

It depends on where your photos live. PicDock covers mixed cloud and NAS sources plus clock, weather and alarm (no Google Drive). PhotoFrame 2 has the widest source list including Google Drive. Fravora and Pixette focus on NAS and self-hosted servers. Snapwood's apps cover one cloud service each. Frameo is for Frameo hardware frames, and FrameOS is for DIY Raspberry Pi frames.

Which Fotoo alternative supports Google Drive?

Of the apps in this list, PhotoFrame 2's listing names Google Drive. PicDock, Fravora, Pixette and the Snapwood apps covered here do not list it.

Can I move my Fotoo settings or albums to another app?

No import exists in any of these apps. Your photos stay where they are, so you sign in to the same sources and choose the same albums or folders again.

How much does PicDock cost compared with Fotoo?

PicDock is free to download with all sources except web upload free; Premium is lifetime (US$19.99), yearly or monthly. Fotoo's US listing shows in-app purchases of $24.99–$39.99 per item (checked October 25, 2026).
